# Behavioural Question Bank

Question families grouped by the competency they test. One strong story per cluster covers almost anything.

# Strategy: ~8 stories, one per cluster

Most behavioral questions are the **same competency in different words.** "Tell me about a time you took ownership," "your biggest accomplishment," and "a project you drove start to finish" all want the *same* story. So: **build ~8 strong, flexible stories — one per cluster below — and you can answer almost anything.** A great story usually flexes across 2–3 clusters by shifting emphasis, so 8 stories over-covers a 45-min round (4–6 questions + follow-ups).

**Pick ONE real experience per cluster.** The stories that land are the ones you actually lived and remember in detail.

**Write these first** (most coverage, highest veto risk): incident/debugging → ambiguity → conflict/disagree-and-commit → failure/humility → ownership → bias for action. Then emergent leadership and mentoring.

# The clusters

### ⭐ Cluster 1 — Ownership & end-to-end delivery
**Must prove:** you took something from start to finish, owned the hard decisions, and drove it to a measurable outcome — without being hand-held.

**Question family:**
- Tell me about a project you owned from start to finish.
- What's the biggest accomplishment of your career so far?
- Tell me about a time you took full ownership of something.
- Describe the most challenging project you've worked on.
- Tell me about something you built that you're proud of.
- A time you went above and beyond your role's definition.

**Be ready for:** "What was the hardest decision?" · "What would you do differently?" · "What was *your* part vs the team's?" · "How did you measure success?"

---

### ⭐ Cluster 2 — Incident / something broke / debugging under pressure
**Must prove:** structured, hypothesis-driven problem-solving under uncertainty; ownership of a failure; prevention afterward.

**Question family:**
- Tell me about a significant bug or issue in production. How did you handle it?
- Describe a challenging technical problem you solved recently.
- A time you were under high pressure — how did you perform?
- A time something went wrong and you had to fix it.
- Walk me through the hardest debugging you've done.

**Be ready for:** "Which failure mode was hardest and why?" · "What did you do to prevent recurrence?" · "What was your first wrong hypothesis?" · "How did you coordinate across teams?"

---

### ⭐ Cluster 3 — Conflict & disagree-and-commit
**Must prove:** you can disagree with data, stay collaborative, and commit gracefully to a decision that didn't go your way. **This is the cluster that most often sinks people — nail the reframe.**

**Question family:**
- Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague or manager. How did you resolve it?
- A time you proposed an idea but the team disagreed. What did you do?
- Describe working with someone difficult.
- A time you had to get people on the same page about a decision.
- A time you were overruled — how did you handle it?

**Critical framing:** never "I argued and refused to back down." Always: *"We evaluated the options together; I advocated for X with the data; when we went with Y, I committed fully — and here's how it turned out."* Relationship preserved, decision improved.

**Be ready for:** "What if you'd been proven right later?" · "How did the relationship hold up?" · "When do you decide to stop pushing?"

---

### ⭐ Cluster 4 — Ambiguity / no clear direction
**Must prove:** you can operate without a spec — define the problem, make and validate assumptions, and move forward instead of freezing. **The key is showing a *structured* approach to the unknown.**

**Question family:**
- Tell me about a time you faced ambiguity in a project's requirements.
- A time you worked on something with no clear direction.
- How did you handle a vague or poorly-defined problem?
- A time requirements changed last-minute — how did you adapt?
- A time you had to make a decision with incomplete information.

**Structure to show:** "no clarity → I defined the problem / clarified the goal → broke the unknown into testable pieces → made a reasonable assumption, validated it, iterated → here's the outcome." Show the *process*, not just that it worked out.

**Be ready for:** "How did you know your assumption was right?" · "What if it had been wrong?" · "How did you decide where to start?"

---

### Cluster 5 — Bias for action / initiative
**Must prove:** you saw a problem nobody assigned you and acted — shipped something, improved something — without waiting for permission.

**Question family:**
- Tell me about a time you took initiative without being asked.
- A time you improved a process or system. What was the impact?
- A time you saw a problem and fixed it proactively.
- An initiative you took that made an impact (for a user/team).

**Be ready for:** "How did you get others to adopt it?" · "How did you find the time?" · "What was the measurable impact?"

---

### Cluster 6 — Influence without authority / emergent leadership
**Must prove:** you led without a title — drove alignment, stepped into an unowned gap, brought people along with data — *and ceded control when it was on track*.

**Question family:**
- Tell me about a time you demonstrated leadership without being the manager.
- A time you got people aligned / drove a cross-team decision.
- A time you stepped into a gap nobody owned.
- A time you influenced others to adopt your approach.
- A time you unblocked or led a team through something.

**Be ready for:** "How did you get buy-in without authority?" · "Did you hand it off afterward?" (say yes — the cede-control half scores high) · "How did you handle people who disagreed?"

---

### ⭐ Cluster 7 — Failure / being wrong / receiving tough feedback (humility)
**Must prove:** you can own a mistake cleanly, learn from it, and treat feedback as a gift. **The absence of this is a top red flag — you MUST have a genuine failure story.**

**Question family:**
- Tell me about the last time you failed. What happened?
- A time you received critical feedback. How did you respond?
- A time you were wrong about something.
- A time you missed a goal — what would you do differently?
- A mistake you made and what you learned.

**Be ready for:** "What did you change afterward?" · "How did it feel?" · "Has it happened again?" (→ no, because you changed X).

**Avoid:** fake weaknesses ("I work too hard"), and any story where the "failure" was actually someone else's fault.

---

### Cluster 8 — Mentoring / developing others
**Must prove:** you invest in people, meet them where they are, and create an outcome *for them*.

**Question family:**
- How do you mentor junior team members? A successful example.
- A time you helped an underperforming teammate improve.
- A time you advocated for someone on your team.
- A challenge you faced while mentoring.
- A time you helped someone learn something new.

**Be ready for:** "What did *they* achieve?" · "How did you adapt to their level?" · "What was hard about it?"

---

### (Bonus) Cluster 9 — Doing the right thing / integrity
**Must prove:** you chose the harder-correct path over the easier-expedient one. Often *folds into Cluster 2* (the symptom-vs-root-cause call).

**Question family:**
- A time you chose the harder right over the easier wrong.
- A time you faced an ethical dilemma at work.
- A time you pushed back on something even though it was inconvenient.
- A time you prioritized doing it correctly over doing it fast.

---

### (Bonus) Cluster 10 — Competing priorities / deadlines
**Must prove:** you triage by impact, communicate, and deliver under constraint. Often *folds into Cluster 1 or 2*.

**Question family:**
- How do you handle multiple competing priorities? A time you juggled several.
- A time you managed a critical project under a tight deadline.
- What would you do if you were going to miss a deadline?
- A time your work was deprioritized mid-project.

---

# Practice questions (Google G&L style)

Interviewers are often deliberately neutral/poker-faced — don't read the room, just deliver structured answers.

**Ownership / initiative / quality**
- Tell me about your biggest career accomplishment.
- Describe a challenging situation at work and how you handled it.
- A time you took ownership of something end-to-end.
- How do you ensure code quality (yours and the team's)? An example where quality focus made a difference.
- A significant production bug/issue — how did you handle it?
- Share an initiative you took for a customer/user that had impact.
- A time you improved a process or system; what was the impact?

**Ambiguity / change / learning**
- A time you faced ambiguity in project requirements.
- A time you had to quickly learn a new technology/tool — your approach and outcome.
- "You're put on an entirely new tech stack with a new team — how do you approach it?" Follow-up: "…and there's no documentation to ramp up on. Now what?"
- A time your work was deprioritized mid-project — how did you handle it? Follow-up: how would it change if you were the team lead?
- A time you had to make last-minute changes to your code — how did you feel?

**Conflict / collaboration / influence**
- A disagreement with a colleague or manager — how you resolved it and the outcome.
- A time you proposed an idea but the team disagreed — how you handled it.
- A time you got people aligned on a decision.
- Working with someone difficult / outside your team — describe it.
- How do you make sure you're being inclusive of everyone's perspectives?

**Feedback / growth**
- A time you received critical feedback from a manager — your response and the actions you took.
- A time you misunderstood requirements — how you rectified it and what you learned.

**Mentoring / leadership**
- How do you mentor junior members? A successful example. Challenges you've faced mentoring.
- A junior member needs additional training — how do you help?
- A time you advocated for yourself or someone on your team.
- A time you helped an underperforming teammate improve.

**Goals / prioritization / "manager" hypotheticals**
- A goal you set and how you approached achieving it. (And: a time you **missed** a personal goal — what you'd do differently.)
- How do you manage multiple competing priorities? A time you juggled several at once.
- What would you do if you were going to miss a deadline?
- "As a manager building a team of 10, how many SDE-1/2/3 would you hire and why?"
- What does your ideal team look like? As manager, how would you protect that setup?

**Googley situational / values**
- Plan a team event where people have different, misaligned preferences — how do you decide? (Bring in **inclusivity**.)
- How do you ensure **accessibility** in the work you do?
- An incident that changed your perception of someone.
- "You're a PM; after all approvals, a friend suggests a helpful change — what do you do?"

# Googleyness traits as questions

| Trait | Typical question | What's being probed |
|---|---|---|
| Comfort with ambiguity | A time you worked on something with no clear direction / a vague problem. | You defined the problem, made and validated assumptions, and moved instead of freezing. |
| Humility & valuing feedback | A time you received tough feedback. / A time you were wrong. | Ego-free growth; dropping your own hypothesis fast when the data disagreed. |
| Bias for action | A time you took initiative without being asked. | You saw a recurring cost nobody owned and shipped a fix. |
| Doing the right thing | A time you chose the harder right over the easier wrong. | E.g. doing the full root cause instead of masking a symptom because correctness mattered. |
| Taking ownership | Something went wrong and you owned it / a production incident you handled. | Diagnosis → fix → prevention, told blamelessly. |
| High standards | A time you set an ambitious goal / raised the bar. | You built for what it would need to survive, not just "works". |
| Thinking freely | A time you solved a problem in an unconventional way. | The creative leap, and why it worked. |
| Collaboration | A time you worked across teams / resolved a conflict. | Credit to others, disagree-and-commit, the relationship preserved. |

# "About you" openers

- **Tell me about yourself** — 60–90 s arc: background → what you do now → the work you're proudest of → why this role is the natural next step. End question-friendly.
- **Why this company / why this role** — specific, not flattering; tie it to something you've actually done.
- **Strengths / weaknesses** — a real weakness *with a correction in motion* ("I used to dive into fixing before aligning stakeholders; now I write a short problem-framing doc first"). No humblebrags.

# Other companies' behavioural questions

**Citadel Securities**

- "Tell me about a time you made a **critical mistake in production** — how did you resolve it?"
- "Describe a time you had to **defend a technical decision against a senior stakeholder**."
- "How do you handle **ambiguity** when requirements aren't fully defined?"
- "**Why Citadel Securities rather than a traditional tech company?**"

They will **cross-examine your CV line by line** — every project, its architecture, failure modes, why you chose each data structure. Re-read your resume beforehand. Don't overstate anything.

**Apple — scope follow-ups**

- A time you cut scope. What did you cut and who was unhappy?
- A time scope grew and you let it. Why was that right?
- How do you handle a stakeholder who wants everything?
- How do you know when something is done?
- A time you disagreed with your manager on scope. *(Disagree-then-commit is a named expectation.)*
