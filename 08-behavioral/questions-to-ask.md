# Questions to Ask

# Questions for interviewers (end of a round)

Indifference is a documented failure mode at several companies — have 4–5 ready, tailored to the interviewer's role.

**SRE / infra teams**
- "How does this team balance reliability work against feature/launch pressure — how do error budgets actually play out in practice?"
- "What does on-call look like here, and how do you keep toil in check?"
- "What's the split between software engineering and systems/ops work day to day?"
- "What's a hard reliability problem the team has tackled recently?" *(engineers love telling war stories — and it's genuinely useful intel)*
- "How do you measure an SRE's impact and growth here?"
- "What surprised you most about the job when you joined?"

**Trading firms**
- "How much of the systems team's day is Python tooling vs. dropping into C++ or lower-level debugging?"
- "How much do you interact directly with the trading side day to day?"
- "What does on-call and incident response look like — how do you do observability and postmortems?"
- "What's the boundary between SRE and the trading-systems developers?"
- "How does the team think about build-vs-buy for internal tooling?"

**Recruiter, before a team-driven loop (e.g. Apple)**
- Which team exactly, and what do they actually build?
- Is the design session mobile/on-device or distributed backend?
- Is there a language preference for the coding rounds?
- For a complete-the-implementation round — their codebase or a standalone problem, and which language?
- What level is this being considered at?
- Can you share the interviewers' names and teams? *(a standard request coordinators usually answer)*

# Team-matching calls

Here's your arsenal of questions, organized by category to help you systematically evaluate each team:

### Team Culture & Work Environment

* "What does a typical week look like for a PE on your team?"
* "How does the team balance firefighting versus project work?"
* "How is on-call structured, and what's the typical pager load?"
* "How autonomous are engineers in choosing what to work on?"
* "What's the most challenging technical problem the team has solved recently?"
* "How does the team celebrate wins?"
* "What's the balance between collaborative work and independent projects?"
* "How does the team approach documentation and knowledge sharing?"

### Technical Growth & Learning

* "What technologies or skills would I be expected to learn in my first 6 months?"
* "How does the team stay current with industry trends and new technologies?"
* "What are the technical challenges that keep your team up at night?"
* "What unique technical skills would I develop on this team that might be harder to get elsewhere?"
* "How much time do engineers typically get for learning and exploration?"
* "What's the most interesting thing someone on your team has built recently?"
* "Do you have any examples of projects where someone joined not knowing the tech stack but was able to ramp up successfully?"

### Career Growth & Advancement

* "How do you measure success for new engineers in their first year?"
* "What have career paths looked like for recent grads who joined your team?"
* "How often do engineers on your team get promoted?"
* "Can you share examples of team members who've grown into leadership roles?"
* "What skills or achievements typically differentiate those who get promoted quickly?"
* "How do you support engineers who want to become technical experts versus those aiming for management?"
* "What opportunities exist to present work externally at conferences or through publications?"
* "How do performance reviews work, and what feedback mechanisms exist?"

### Team Impact & Visibility

* "How does your team's work directly impact the company's business metrics?"
* "What's the most significant way your team has influenced the company's infrastructure recently?"
* "How visible is your team's work to senior leadership?"
* "How does your team collaborate with other organizations?"
* "What major projects are on your roadmap for the next year?"
* "How much interaction would I have with product teams or business stakeholders?"
* "What challenges do you anticipate your team facing in the next 1-2 years?"

### Work-Life Balance & Support

* "How does the team manage work during high-pressure periods?"
* "What's your approach to vacation and time off?"
* "How flexible are working hours on your team?"
* "What support systems exist for engineers who are struggling with a problem?"
* "How do you prevent burnout during intense project periods?"
* "What are the in-office expectations?"

### Manager-Specific Questions

* "What's your leadership philosophy?"
* "How do you help engineers grow and develop?"
* "What qualities do you value most in your team members?"
* "How do you handle disagreements within the team?"
* "How often do you have 1:1s with your reports?"
* "What's been your proudest moment leading this team?"
* "How would your team describe your management style?"

### Subtle but Powerful Questions

* "What keeps you here, specifically on this team?" (Reveals true motivations)
* "What surprised you the most when you joined this team?" (Uncovers hidden realities)
* "If you could change one thing about how the team operates, what would it be?" (Exposes pain points)
* "How has the team evolved over the past year?" (Shows trajectory)
* "What types of engineers tend to thrive here versus struggle?" (Culture fit check)
* "What's something the team is still figuring out?" (Honesty check)
* "How does the team handle technical disagreements?" (Decision-making culture)
* "What opportunities have team members had to rotate to other teams or projects?" (Mobility)

### Closing Power Questions

* "Based on my background, where do you see me making the biggest impact on your team?"
* "What would success look like for me in my first 6 months?"
* "Is there anything about my background or experience that gives you hesitation about my fit for this role?"
* "What would make me your top choice among candidates you're speaking with?"

Remember to adapt these questions based on the conversation flow and what matters most to you. Ask follow-up questions when you get interesting answers, and pay attention to not just what they say, but how they say it.
